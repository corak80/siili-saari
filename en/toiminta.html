Our work

From rescue to release

Every hedgehog follows the same path. Here is how it goes — from the moment it is found to the morning it disappears back under a bush.

Found a hedgehog? Keep it warm and quiet in a box and call us. Do not give it milk.
Call now +358 6 321 7300

1. Rescue and first aid

Urgent intake and stabilisation. A hedgehog that has just been found is almost always cold and dehydrated, and that is treated first: warmth, fluids and quiet. Only once it is stable is a closer examination worthwhile. Triage happens on arrival.

2. Veterinary treatment

Full diagnostics and treatment under veterinary supervision: radiography, surgery, wound care, parasite treatment and medication protocols. The most common reasons for admission are mower injuries, dog bites, entanglement in netting, flystrike, and poor condition caused by parasites.

3. Rehabilitation and release

Gradual recovery in naturalistic outdoor enclosures. Weight is monitored, foraging ability is assessed, and human contact is kept to a minimum so the hedgehog does not become tame. Release is into suitable habitat — preferably where the hedgehog was found.

4. Advice and education

Community outreach, school visits and public awareness of hedgehog conservation. Most of the hedgehogs that end up in care could have been spared: check the bush before mowing, remove netting, leave a gap in the fence. We publish two guides anyone can use.

  • Hedgehog care guide — when a hedgehog needs help, first aid and feeding orphans.
  • Build a hedgehog house — dimensions, materials, placement and a care calendar.

The year round

The hedgehog year is uneven. In spring, animals emerging from hibernation are thin and vulnerable. Summer brings the injuries: mowers, dogs, netting. From late summer into autumn the focus shifts to juveniles — animals too small to survive hibernation without help. In winter, those in care are typically the ones that did not grow enough in time.

Volunteers

Rehabilitation is hands-on work: feeding, cleaning, weighing and record-keeping, every single day. Volunteers are trained for the role, and everyone starts with an induction before their first shift.

How you can help

Right now what we need most is hands, lifts, and gardens that are safe for hedgehogs.
